Business Models

Three models that work at manageable small scale (≤6–7 residents). This size maximizes zoning protections and keeps staffing realistic for an owner-operator.

Senior Living / Board & Care

Home-like care for seniors needing help with daily activities, meds, and meals. Longer stays and strong private-pay potential.

In-Home Care Agency

Care delivered in the client’s own home. No facility to license. Faster start, but requires ongoing sales and caregiver management.

Sober Living

Peer-supported, substance-free housing for recovery. Structure and accountability without clinical treatment on-site.

Overall Analysis

California has strong demand from both aging demographics and the recovery pipeline, but also the highest real-estate costs and very uneven local zoning. The ≤6–7 resident size remains the practical sweet spot for facility-based models. North Carolina (especially Wake County) currently offers a lower-capital alternative with simpler statewide rules for non-clinical sober living.

Final guidance: Match the model to your capital, timeline, and willingness to manage care versus housing. For most first-time operators, In-Home Care or non-clinical Sober Living at ≤6 residents are the more accessible on-ramps. Senior Board & Care rewards those prepared for full licensing with higher per-resident revenue and longer stays.

Plan of Action — Raleigh / Wake County Market Research

Plan of Action — Raleigh / Wake County Market Research

If you are targeting sober living in the Raleigh area, start with these three organizations. They give the highest-signal insights on demand, referral standards, and real-world supply gaps.

OC Links (Orange County Health Care Agency – Behavioral Health)

Main county behavioral health information and referral line. They handle substance-use referrals, can describe the local recovery-housing landscape, and point you toward contracted recovery residence providers and county expectations.

What you can learn:

  • Monthly / quarterly placement volume into sober living
  • Preferred house characteristics (size, rules, manager model, MAT-friendly status)
  • What makes them recommend one residence over another
  • Current gaps in local supply
Contact: (855) 625-4657 (24/7)

Access and Crisis Line (San Diego County Behavioral Health Services)

Primary county entry point for mental health and substance-use services. Operators and researchers use this line to understand referral pathways, residential treatment discharge patterns, and how the county connects people to recovery housing.

What you can learn:

  • Monthly / quarterly placement volume into sober living
  • Preferred house characteristics (size, rules, manager model, MAT-friendly status)
  • What makes them recommend one residence over another
  • Current gaps in local supply
Contact: (888) 724-7240

Second Chance (Drug Court / Re-Entry Programs)

Operates drug-court and re-entry related services in San Diego. Useful for understanding court-ordered or justice-involved demand for sober living and what standards those programs look for in housing partners.

Contact: (619) 234-8888

1. Wake County Recovery Court

Why contact them first: Participants can be court-ordered into a sober living environment. This is one of the most direct and structured sources of demand in the county.

What you can learn:

  • Monthly / quarterly placement volume into sober living
  • Preferred house characteristics (size, rules, manager model, MAT-friendly status)
  • What makes them recommend one residence over another
  • Current gaps in local supply
Location: Wake County Courthouse, 4th Floor, Suite 412
316 Fayetteville St, Raleigh, NC 27601

2. SouthLight Healthcare (Raleigh Hub)

Why contact them: Major local treatment provider (outpatient, MAT/opioid treatment, intensive outpatient). They are also the substance-use treatment provider for Wake County Recovery Court, so they sit at the intersection of clinical care and court-involved clients who need step-down housing.

What you can learn:

  • Volume of clients discharging who need sober living
  • Typical client profiles and length-of-stay preferences
  • What referral partners actually look for in a recovery residence
  • Pain points with existing local housing options
Location: 2101 Garner Rd, Raleigh, NC 27610
Phone: 919-787-6131

3. Emmaus House of Raleigh (or Fellowship Home of Raleigh)

Why contact them: Established, operating recovery residences in Raleigh. Both appear as named partners in Wake County’s opioid-settlement Recovery Housing Support funding. Talking to an actual operator gives ground-level reality on occupancy, rates, resident mix, competition (including Oxford Houses), and day-to-day challenges.

What you can learn:

  • Real occupancy patterns and pricing
  • Resident profiles and common challenges
  • How they interact with courts and treatment providers
  • Where the biggest local gaps currently sit
Emmaus House starting point: 10 N. East Street, Raleigh, NC 27601
Phone: 919-327-2229
Alternative: Fellowship Home of Raleigh (Men’s: 919-833-6030)
Suggested sequence:
1. Wake County Recovery Court (demand + standards)
2. SouthLight Healthcare (treatment discharge pipeline)
3. Emmaus House or Fellowship Home (real-world supply & operations)

Secondary options if needed: Recovery Communities of North Carolina (RCNC) for peer/advocacy perspective, or Healing Transitions for peer-run program insights.
